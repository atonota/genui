Radius options

The fully rounded option uses pill corners. Its CSS equivalent is 9999 px to avoid elliptical corners on wide buttons.

Border options

Both options share the radius and opacity decisions. The border is part of the design; focus adds no surrounding ring.

Size and padding

Base heights: 44, 48, 56 and 64 px; inline padding: 17, 25, 30 and 24 px. Text is at least 1rem; long labels and zoom may increase the body height.

Width options

Approved: Content width, Wide and Full width. Narrow remains experimental.

Letter spacing options

Approved: 0 px / natural. All buttons use the same letter spacing.

Opacity and interaction

Normal opacity is 0.9; hover is 1.0. The body does not move on hover. Disabled states preserve their accessibility behavior.

States

Approved: Normal, Hovered, Pressed, Disabled, Processing, Saving, Syncing and Queued. Light and dark appearances share the same state contract.

Ant Design implementation contract

Product components use Ant Design Button and ConfigProvider. Radius, sizing, opacity and non-surrounding keyboard focus decisions are preserved. The 44 / 48 / 56 px heights map to the three standard sizes; 64 px uses the shared component style. Fully rounded buttons use shape="round".

Colors come from Ant Design palettes. Shadows and the other five operation states are experiments. Eight approved button states are recorded in the decision list. These standalone HTML previews do not install the React / antd runtime. The JSON recipe includes theme tokens, Button props and the required shared style mapping.

04 / Visual behavior
Appearance is a state machine.
The buttons below show 13 states of the same design. Interaction communicates through opacity, depth and timing, alongside color. The outer top edge and label stay fixed while the lower-lip surface extends downward by 4 px.
Hover and touch
Hover occurs while a pointer rests over a control. Touch screens cannot rely on it. Select Hovered manually to compare the same visual state on a phone.
Press and focus
Pressed is temporary feedback, not a persistent selection. Button keyboard focus uses one straight leading marker without underlining the label or surrounding the body. The marker does not move the layout.
Transitions and animations
A transition smooths a change between states. An animation follows its own timeline. Easing controls acceleration and deceleration; equal durations can feel different.
Depth language
Use subtle soft shadows or a thin lower layer. The outer top edge and label stay fixed. Press extends the lower surface from 44 to 48 px, covering the stationary lower layer. A 2 px upper layer then appears inside the body with matching rounded corners. Release reveals the lower strip again. No gradients or inset shadows.
Operation feedback
Processing, saving, syncing, queued, offline, warning, retry, success and error states also change the label. Color alone does not convey meaning. The atlas simulates operations visually; no data is sent.
Motion preference
The system reduced-motion preference and animation toggle are respected. Press, focus and state colors remain visible when motion is disabled.
Simple designs can still communicate richly.Choose one visual identity, consistent focus and a few interaction rules for a product family. Combining every option does not make every option useful.

What does this number count?
The mathematical product of the options in this file, rather than distinct, usable or accessible buttons.
  • Some choices look similar. Geometry is limited to rectangles and tabs, with 0, 3, 5 px or pill corners.
  • Some border choices look similar on borderless surfaces. Differences depend on the color and background.
  • Custom colors can affect contrast; verify readability on the chosen surface and background.
  • Identical surfaces can behave differently with different easing and durations. Still screenshots do not show behavior.
  • Size, color and duration are design decisions; radius and border choices in this atlas are limited to approved variants. The real design space has no universal finite upper bound.
Therefore: Without defining the options, there is no single answer to “how many different buttons are possible?”.
How do you narrow this for one product?
Choose a surface family, corner style and depth language first. Then define a few emphasis levels and behavior recipes covering every state. Use the atlas to explore; avoid random styles across product screens.
